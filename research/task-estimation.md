# Task Estimation in Scrum - Research

## Source 1 - Mike Cohn

Link: https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/agile/agile-estimation

Notes:
- Focus is on estimating the relative size of tasks rather than exact time
- Story points are used to compare work instead of predicting hours
- Teams tend to get better at estimating as they gain experience

Key Insight:
Estimation works better when treated as a rough comparison between tasks rather than trying to be precise


## Source 2 - Martin Fowler

Link: https://martinfowler.com/bliki/Estimation.html

Notes:
- Estimating software is difficult because there are always unknowns
- Requirements can change while the work is being done
- Early estimates are often inaccurate

Key Insight:
Estimates are unreliable by nature because software development involves a lot of uncertainty


## Source 3 - Atlassian

Link: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/estimation

Notes:
- Estimation should be done as a team rather than individually
- Techniques like planning poker help encourage discussion
- Better communication leads to better estimates

Key Insight:
Estimation tends to be more accurate when it is done collaboratively instead of by one person


## Source 4 - Troy Magennis

Link: https://focusedobjective.com/

Notes:
- Story points are often misunderstood by teams
- Sometimes they are treated like fixed measurements
- This can make estimation less useful than intended

Key Insight:
Using estimation techniques incorrectly can reduce their effectiveness rather than improve it


## Source 5 - Basecamp

Link: https://basecamp.com/

Notes:
- Estimation is difficult because not everything is known at the start
- Teams often underestimate how complex tasks actually are
- Changing requirements can affect timelines significantly

Key Insight:
Estimation often fails because of hidden complexity and changing scope


## Common Themes
 
- Estimation is inherently uncertain
- Teams frequently underestimate work
- Collaboration improves estimation accuracy
- Estimation techniques are often misused
- Estimates should not be treated as fixed commitments
